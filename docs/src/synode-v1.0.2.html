1 · Overview

Synode is a single-scroll fashion photography journal for synode.space. There is no main menu: the page reads top to bottom like a manuscript, in seven movements. Depth comes from slow parallax on photographs, sticky captions that hold while plates drift past, and stacked leaves that settle over one another. Everything else is restraint: parchment ground, ink type, hairline gold rules and one rust accent.

The reading column is capped at 62ch. Display type receives a negative right margin equal to its tracking so centred capitals are optically, not mathematically, centred.

Layout

  • Folio grid — three columns: gloss margin (min 9rem), reading column (max 40rem), and a deliberately empty outer margin 1.6× wider. Padding is 5% left, 10% right on desktop, collapsing to max(16px, 6%) below 960px.
  • Sticky chapter — caption column is position: sticky; top: 18vh; plates sit on a 6-column sub-grid with alternating offsets (1–5, 3–7, 1–7).
  • Stacked leaves — each leaf is a 100svh sticky container; leaves 2 and 3 rest 1.25rem lower so the previous edge stays visible. On phones (<720px) stacking is switched off and leaves simply follow each other.
  • Contact sheet — 12 columns with negative and positive margin offsets for an asymmetric, hand-laid feel; two columns on phones.

Motion

  • Parallax writes a single custom property, --py, consumed by transform: translate3d(). Only transforms animate, so scrolling stays on the compositor.
  • An IntersectionObserver keeps only on-screen layers in the update set; updates are batched in requestAnimationFrame. Native scrolling is never intercepted.
  • Speeds are deliberately small: 0.02–0.12 for plates, 0.2–0.35 for backgrounds. Background layers are oversized (inset: -12% / -25%) so no edge can show.
  • Text blocks fade up 1.5rem over 900ms on cubic-bezier(.22,.61,.36,1), once.
  • prefers-reduced-motion disables parallax, reveal, the scroll cue and image zoom, and turns off smooth scrolling.

3 · Elegance review

Review of the pasted Hellenic Manuscript guide as a system for a fashion photography site, with what v1.0.1 already does about each point.

Palette restraint & temperature9 / 10 Type pairing (display / section / body)8 / 10 Accessibility of the palette as specified6 / 10 Spacing & manuscript proportion9 / 10 Economy (number of typefaces)6 / 10 Overall, as written8 / 10

What is already elegant

  • Warm, not white. Ink on parchment gives 16.4:1 contrast without the glare of #000 on #FFF. Photography reads richer on a warm ground, and the parchment tint lets plates float.
  • The 60/30/10 split maps naturally onto a scroll page: parchment for reading, ink for the three cinematic bands (hero, interlude, colophon), rust and gold only as points of light.
  • Lapidary capitals + Didone + Garamond is a historically coherent trio: Roman inscription for titles, eighteenth-century high contrast for sections, Renaissance text face for reading.
  • Hairline gold at 40% opacity is the right strength: it reads as ruling on a page, not as decoration.
  • Asymmetric margins give the photographs room and stop the page from looking like a template.

The one real problem in the guide: it assigns Hellenic Gold to “metadata”. On parchment, gold text is 2.38:1 and unreadable for many people. v1.0.1 resolves this by setting meta in Laurel on light grounds and allowing gold text only on Aegean Ink (.meta--gold, hero, colophon). Gold on parchment is used for rules only.

Refinements recommended for v1.1

  1. Fewer voices. The guide calls on six families (Cinzel, Cinzel Decorative, Bodoni Moda, GFS Didot, EB Garamond, Spectral). EB Garamond's own italic is one of the finest and could replace Spectral; Cinzel Decorative can be subset to the few drop-cap letters with Google Fonts' &text= parameter. That cuts roughly a third of the font payload and tightens the page's voice.
  2. “Thick, high-contrast” headers. Cinzel is an inscriptional face with low stroke contrast; the high contrast lives in Bodoni Moda. The guide's wording conflates the two. Consider Cinzel 700 instead of 900 for the hero: at 72px with +0.15em tracking, 900 starts to look heavy beside fine photography.
  3. Didone tracking. +0.05em suits all-caps, but loosens lowercase Bodoni. For title-case H2s, +0.01–0.02em keeps the hairlines crisp. (This document already uses +0.02em for its H2s for comparison.)
  4. Thera White vs parchment. Pure #FFF next to #F7F4EE can read slightly cool. Keep it for the stacked leaves, where the lift is intentional, but don't spread it to other surfaces.
  5. GFS Didot should stay for Greek text only (as in the hero's Συνέδριον). For Latin H2s, Bodoni Moda has the better Latin glyphs and optical sizes.
  6. Rust on ink (3.01:1) works for the colophon button, where white text sits on a rust fill, but never set rust text directly on ink below 24px.

5 · Upload & update guide

Should I just drop the folder? Yes. Filenames are fixed, so replacing the files in images/plates/ is enough for the photos to appear. You only touch index.html to rewrite captions and alt text, and to bump the cache version.

Step by step

  1. Choose. Pick one photograph per slot. Proportions close to the “Ratio” column crop best; frames are cropped around the centre.
  2. Export web copies (Lightroom / Capture One): JPEG, sRGB, quality 75–82, long edge as listed. Metadata: All except Camera & Camera Raw info, with Remove Location Info ticked. This keeps your copyright and drops GPS. Never upload originals.
  3. Stamp rights metadata (if your export preset doesn't already). See §6.
  4. Rename exactly as catalogued: lowercase, .jpg.
  5. Drop the files into images/plates/, overwriting the placeholders. Put the share card in images/og/.
  6. Rewrite text. In index.html, at the lines in the catalogue, update each alt="…" (what the photo shows, for screen readers and search) and its caption.
  7. Bump the cache version. Find and replace ?v=1.0.2 with ?v=1.0.3 (and so on each time). Without this, returning visitors may keep seeing old files.
  8. Preview locally: python -m http.server in the site folder, then open http://localhost:8000.
  9. Upload to the web root of synode.space via your host's file manager, SFTP or Git deploy.

6 · Image protection: the technical boundary

Plain truth first. Anything a browser can display has already been downloaded to the visitor's device, and anyone can take a screenshot. No website can make images impossible to copy. What a site can do is stop casual saving, keep high-resolution files off the web, make ownership provable, and opt out of AI training in a form the law recognises. v1.0.2 does all four.

Deliberately not done

  • Site-wide right-click blocking. It breaks copying text, opening links in new tabs and some accessibility tools, and protects nothing extra. Blocking is limited to photographs.
  • Canvas / base64 obfuscation. Slower pages, worse search ranking, and still defeated by a screenshot.
  • Visible watermarks. Off by default for elegance. If you want one, a small © in a corner of your highest-value plates, added at export, is the least intrusive form.

Stamping metadata with ExifTool

exiftool -overwrite_original -gps:all= \
  -Artist="[Your name]" -XMP-dc:Creator="[Your name]" \
  -Copyright="© 2026 [Your name]. All rights reserved." \
  -IPTC:CopyrightNotice="© 2026 [Your name]. All rights reserved." \
  -XMP-xmpRights:Marked=True \
  -XMP-xmpRights:WebStatement="https://synode.space/terms.html" \
  -XMP-xmpRights:UsageTerms="No reproduction or AI training without written licence." \
  -XMP-plus:LicensorURL="https://synode.space/terms.html" \
  -XMP-plus:DataMining="DMI-PROHIBITED" \
  images/plates/*.jpg

Lightroom equivalent: a Metadata preset with Copyright, Copyright Status = Copyrighted, Rights Usage Terms and Copyright Info URL. Photoshop and Lightroom can also attach Content Credentials (C2PA), a signed record that you made the image.

If your host is not Apache

# nginx — inside server { }
location ~* \.(jpe?g|png|webp|avif)$ {
  valid_referers none blocked synode.space www.synode.space;
  if ($invalid_referer) { return 403; }
  add_header X-Robots-Tag "noai, noimageai";
  add_header tdm-reservation "1";
}
location /docs/ { deny all; }

Cloudflare (any host): Scrape Shield → Hotlink Protection on; Security → Bots → Block AI bots on. Netlify / Vercel / GitHub Pages ignore .htaccess; set the same headers in their config files, and rely on Cloudflare for hotlink protection.

Watching for misuse

Search your key plates now and then with Google Lens or TinEye, or use a monitoring service (Pixsy, Copytrack) that finds copies and handles claims. Optional: tools such as Glaze add near-invisible changes that disrupt AI style mimicry, at a small cost in image quality.

7 · Intellectual property: the legal boundary

General information, not legal advice. Law varies by country; have a lawyer in your jurisdiction review the terms before launch, especially if you plan to license images commercially.

What you own automatically

Copyright in a photograph belongs to the photographer the moment it is taken, in all 181 countries of the Berne Convention, with no registration or © symbol required. If someone else was hired to shoot for Synode, ownership follows their contract: get a written assignment or licence from them.

What strengthens it

  • US registration. To sue over a US work you must first register it with the US Copyright Office. Registering before infringement (or within three months of publication) also unlocks statutory damages and legal fees, which is usually what makes a claim worth pursuing. Published photos can be registered in groups of up to 750 under one application (GRPPH).
  • Copyright management information. Removing or altering embedded rights metadata is separately unlawful in the US (17 U.S.C. § 1202) and the EU (InfoSoc Directive Art. 7). That is why §6 stamps it into every file.
  • AI training opt-out. In the EU, Article 4(3) of the DSM Directive lets rights-holders reserve their works from text and data mining, but for online content only in machine-readable form. robots.txt, tdmrep.json and the tdm-reservation meta and header provide exactly that. The EU AI Act requires general-purpose AI providers to respect such reservations.
  • Records. Keep RAW files, shoot dates and model releases. They are your proof of authorship.

What you must secure from others

  • Model releases for every recognisable person, covering web publication and, if you plan to license images, use by third parties.
  • Designers and brands. Editorial display of garments is generally fine. Commercial licensing of images that feature a brand's logo or distinctive design may need that brand's consent.
  • Locations. Some archaeological sites (including on Delos) require permits for professional photography; keep them on file.

Where terms of use fit

Copyright is the primary protection; the Terms of Use add a contract layer that states plainly what is forbidden (downloading, AI training, hotlinking, metadata removal) and how to get a licence. Terms shown by a footer link (“browsewrap”) are weaker contracts than terms a visitor clicks to accept, but they remove any claim of innocent use and support takedown notices.

When you find a copy

  1. Screenshot the use with URL and date.
  2. Send a takedown notice to the host or platform (DMCA in the US; Digital Services Act notice in the EU). Most platforms have a form.
  3. For commercial misuse, a licence-fee demand through a lawyer or a monitoring service is common practice.

Privacy note

The page loads fonts from Google Fonts, which passes visitors' IP addresses to Google. A German court (LG München, 2022) held this unlawful without consent under GDPR. If you expect EU visitors, self-hosting the fonts is the safer path; the Terms disclose the current setup. If your host adds analytics or cookies, update Terms §9.

9 · Production notes

Tonal filter

A light tonal filter (saturate(.82) sepia(.12)) pulls mixed sources toward the palette. If your grading is already final, remove it in layout.css §1. For further performance, add AVIF/WebP versions with srcset later; the fixed-filename JPGs remain the fallback.

Adding a parallax layer

<figure class="plate" data-speed="0.06">   positive = slower than scroll, negative = faster
  <div class="plate__frame"><img …></div>
  <figcaption class="caption"><span class="caption__no">11</span> …</figcaption>
</figure>

Background layers need headroom: inset ≥ speed × (viewport + section height) / 2.

Browser support

Evergreen Chromium, Firefox and Safari. color-mix(), svh, text-wrap: balance and initial-letter degrade gracefully: fallbacks are provided for viewport units and the drop cap, and balance is cosmetic.
